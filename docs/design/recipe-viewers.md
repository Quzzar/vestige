# Ritual recipe viewers

**Owner direction, October 6, 2026.** Optional recipe-viewer support presents the offerings and their output as an ancient ritual sketch. Four-component spell recipes show four inner Plinths around a central Spellstone; recipes requiring more than four retain those four inner Plinths and add four outer Plinths at the corners. The eight-slot arrangement is two layers, not eight seats on a single circle. Both use the same Plinth type. The output occupies the center, with distinct inward arrows. Each layer has its own faint circular guide passing through its Plinth slot centers. Mostly plain ticks, with a few tiny open bends, suggest whole-layout rotation. These guides remain much quieter than the inward arrows and do not imply that layers may rotate independently. Public and previously crafted spell offerings use hoverable native item icons; uncrafted spell offerings use question marks. No step-by-step instructions, fixed compass labels, shaping coefficients or attunement-signature calculations appear in the drawing.

JEI and EMI have native adapters sharing one diagram and display model. Neither mod is required or bundled. When both are installed, EMI owns the ritual category and the JEI adapter omits its category/recipes so its compatibility bridge does not duplicate them. Ordinary apparatus construction remains vanilla crafting and is handled by each viewer normally. REI has no native adapter in this implementation.

**Category name, October 6.** The owner selected **Spellstone** as the category title in JEI and EMI, matching workstation-style names in other categories. The internal `spellstone_ritual` category identity is retained; earlier screenshots showing “Spellstone Rituals” record the previous title.

**Paper-only artwork, October 6.** The owner requested removing the surrounding Minecraft-style UI from the art preview. Its gray beveled frame and duplicate "Spellstone Rituals" title were mock chrome added by Vestige's preview, not generated art or the native diagram. The current preview removes them and shows the paper alone. Native `RitualDiagram` already draws only the paper, guides, stone sketches and item/question-mark overlays. JEI's category now returns `needsRecipeBorder() == false`, using its public API to avoid an additional per-recipe border around the parchment's own edge. The actual outer window, tabs, navigation, search and workstation controls belong to the installed recipe viewer. The pinned EMI public recipe API does not expose a corresponding background-removal option; its normal host UI remains. This change does not replace either viewer's screen or alter other categories through internal classes or mixins. The paper-only export illustrates Vestige's artwork, not a frameless in-game viewer window.

**Player knowledge and recipe memory, October 6.** Uncrafted spell displays show only their four/eight-slot capacity and an **Unknown Scroll** result until the viewer identifies that spell. Every uncrafted spell Plinth shows a question mark, including unused seats in an eight-slot recipe. Neither occupancy, ingredient names, alternatives nor relative order is revealed. After that player successfully commits an ingredient craft, its actual offerings and empty seats become visible, and usage searches and recipe trees can include its ingredients. Crafting does not identify the spell; identifying it does not teach its recipe. Received scrolls, random fragment discovery, hints, failures and canceled crafts unlock neither record. `R` still looks up the scroll’s base identity. Public recipes always show actual offerings and leave unused Plinths empty.

Actual in-world spell recipes retain their authored relationships: whole quarter-turns are valid, mirrors and independent ring rotations are not. Cosmetic apparatus finishes remain interchangeable workstations. The viewer changes no discovery state, crafting eligibility or ritual hints.

The Attunement Shard display has eight Plinths, with **two separate Amethyst Shards, one Echo Shard, one Iron Ingot, one Diamond and one Lapis Lazuli**, leaving two Plinths empty. The drawn positions are an example of its shapeless acceptance. Its output icon is a generic shard, without an invented key. All actual keys lead to this same recipe. Geometry, ingredient–imbuement pairing and color normalization continue to determine actual crafted signatures on the server; these algorithms are not recipe-viewer instructions. Random fragment discovery and dismantling are not represented as deterministic ritual outputs.

## Source and synchronization

Display entries are derived from `RitualRecipe`; shard offerings come directly from `AttunementShardItem.ingredients()`. Uncrafted spell entries contain no offerings; crafted entries contain the current server recipe’s actual offerings. Each recipient receives only their own identification flags and crafting history, derived from persistent player data. Identified entries carry the current server-authored native rarity; unknown entries carry neutral Common regardless of their actual rarity. Scroll names use the same personal white/yellow/aqua/light-purple palette in inventory and viewers, with every unknown name white. Public offering alternatives can expand installed item IDs and current tag members. Viewer code does not repeat recipe authoring rules or alter the runtime.

The server sends bounded pages of ritual display data during NeoForge's `OnDatapackSyncEvent`, before vanilla recipes/tags are synchronized. Both viewer APIs then rebuild against the completed client snapshot on their normal recipe update. Disconnect clears the snapshot. This covers remote servers and `/reload`, rather than relying on a client-only copy of the packaged catalog. The version-4 payload includes spell identity, identification flag, revealed native rarity and capacity, with an empty offering list for uncrafted spells and actual alternatives for crafted spells. Public entries may carry offering alternatives. Login and datapack reload use the ensuing vanilla recipe update. Identification, first successful ingredient craft, respawn and dimension changes send personalized snapshots with a live refresh flag; on completion EMI rebuilds through paired public NeoForge recipe/tag update events using the current client registry access. Its pinned implementation waits for both events; a recipe event alone leaves the new native inputs unindexed. JEI ignores that event when vanilla recipes are unchanged, so its adapter uses the public runtime APIs to hide obsolete native entries and add their replacements. Identification flags do not replace recipe layouts. JEI ingredient-list UIDs include unknown/identified visibility, while recipe UIDs retain the base spell ID. Only JEI index copies carry a fixed visibility stamp so a name transition removes the old cached UID and adds the new one; playable scrolls and recipe outputs retain their native data. This refreshes cached search text without duplicate recipes or full vanilla recipe synchronization. Repeated identical snapshots add no entries. The client also updates its scroll-name knowledge atomically; disconnect clears both snapshots. It contains no signature blueprint, key or shaping mathematics. This is deliberate UI concealment, not encryption of the distributed recipe data.

## Development and evidence

Use `-Precipe_viewer=jei`, `emi` or `both` for an optional development runtime; the default is `none`. `runEffectsCapture -Pcapture_kind=recipes` creates an isolated flat test world, opens actual viewer recipe screens through their public lookup APIs and exports Minecraft framebuffer captures. Two effective GUI widths cover four-slot spells, eight-slot spells and the shard. EMI supplies a smaller holder on short screens; the adapter scales the complete drawing, native icons and their hover bounds together to fit it. JEI uses the full 184×150 area. Its sidebar shows one Spellstone and one Plinth, while all cosmetic variants remain invisible catalysts for usage lookup. EMI rituals use its documented synthetic-ID prefix because they are not vanilla RecipeManager entries. The capture helpers keep optional APIs isolated from ordinary client event subscribers, allowing either viewer or neither to be installed.

The visual reference pass inspected three ingredient/instruction layouts from Mobbin: [Ghost](https://mobbin.com/screens/ff0ac574-0dbe-494f-bbd5-9329a9580b2d), [Blue Apron](https://mobbin.com/screens/b13e2ebb-7737-41c1-9690-a7ed8215475b) and [Julienne](https://mobbin.com/screens/7dda3d7b-1288-45bc-976e-9755bbc43ebe). They reinforced identifiable ingredients, a clear focal result and restrained disclosure; their long instructional text, numbered steps and cooking photography do not fit this native game interface. The owner's sketch direction superseded an initial dark rectangular layout with a separate result column and explicit instructions.

**Art exploration history.** The owner rejected the procedural parchment/outline drawing on October 6 and requested generated image concepts, followed by trying an image asset in the viewer if the result looks good. That interim procedural drawing is superseded by the approved v7 assets. The exploration compared pale archaeological sketches, engraved manuscript diagrams and dark violet chalk diagrams. Public ingredient icons and the central output remain native, hoverable overlays; the background asset must leave their landing spots clear. Small-screen captures also show that the final diagram must fit a shorter recipe area.

The first generated contact sheet and an interactive art comparison are saved in `docs/previews/ritual-image-concepts/`. That preview uses the generated image as its actual backdrop, with separate item textures at game size and 2×. It is a browser art study, not an in-game presentation verification of image assets.

**Accepted visual direction, October 6.** The owner approved the cleaner archaeological A style, with corner symbols removed and Antique Atlas as a shared visual reference for rituals and Standing Stones. They then corrected the eight-slot geometry to four inner plus four outer Plinths, and requested two faint circular guides with small neutral ticks rather than explicit rotational arrows, while keeping the inward arrows distinct. The current preview shows the corrected four/eight arrangements with Atlas's book asset, Patchouli's actual book screen and vanilla crafting alongside. [UI precedent research](../research/ritual-ui-precedents.md) records the inspected source, distinction between Atlas's own UI and UnionLib, and why a shared Vestige visual style is preferable to adding an unverified UI dependency. Browser art verification and native image-asset verification are separate; the approved generated assets now replace the procedural drawing in both native adapters, verified by the native captures recorded below.

**Alignment refinement, October 6.** The owner requested slight arrow-like bends on some ticks and exact alignment between the circles and slots. Earlier baked composites had unequal cardinal spacing and oversized guides. The latest browser prototype positions transparent generated Plinth/Spellstone illustrations and native items from shared coordinates; its guide circles derive their radii from those same coordinates. The [offline export and audit](../previews/ritual-image-concepts/README.md) confirm alignment for the four-slot circle and both eight-slot circles. Final browser inspection was blocked by the existing error-page tab's unsupported protocol; the offline export is not a browser or native client capture.

**Historical pre-concealment checks:** Native JEI-only and EMI-only inspection each indexed 215 rituals (214 spell recipes plus the shard). Both recognized base and shaped Fireball scrolls, real attuned shards and Lapis usages, and refreshed the Fireball display after an isolated-world datapack override. These checks verify lookup and synchronization, not acceptance of the interim art.

**Final native verification.** [Approved-art captures](../art/recipe-viewers-v8/README.md) record JEI-only, EMI-only and combined-viewer inspection of 215 entries, base/shaped scroll and attuned-shard lookup, zero Paper/Blaze Rod ritual usages, the public Lapis usage and a concealed four-to-eight-slot datapack reload. The small-screen EMI layout now fits its actual holder, with scaled native icons and matching hover bounds. These are Minecraft framebuffer captures, independent of the browser-export limitation above.

**Paper-only follow-up verification.** [Fresh JEI captures](../art/recipe-viewers-v9/README.md) verify the supported per-recipe border removal at both tested GUI sizes, with all lookup, concealment and reload checks retained. They also show the viewer's own surrounding window and controls, which the paper-only preview does not simulate. Java 21 build and all 97 unit tests pass. The EMI adapter and the three generated art assets are unchanged.

**Player-knowledge follow-up verification.** [Fresh native captures](../art/scroll-knowledge-native/README.md) inspect JEI, EMI and both together with 216 ritual entries: 214 spells, Attunement Shard and Homebound Eye. Fireball's successful ingredient craft reveals its actual ingredients and adds one Paper usage without identifying it. Identifying Magnetic Attraction reveals its name while retaining question marks in its uncrafted recipe. Base/shaped lookup remains singular, and JEI's cached name search updates immediately. The combined run retains viewer-bridge warnings and is not a clean general compatibility claim; the archive records their scope and the isolated EMI tooltip-footer setting.
