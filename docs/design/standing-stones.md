# Standing Stones

For the owner-requested October 10 payment-imbuement plan, see [Standing Stone payment imbuements](standing-stone-imbuement-plan.md). The owner approved this complete package; all five routes are implemented.

**Accepted design and partial implementation, October 6, 2026.** The owner selected the name **Standing Stone**, matching-shard networks, **same-dimension travel only**, and originally a map with clickable stones alongside a destination list. **The later accepted menu decision supersedes that map: one clean destination list, an editable current-stone name and the attunement signature at the bottom.** Terrain maps and third-party compatibility remain optional future work. Every linked stone in the source dimension is a peer destination; there is no required hub. Standing Stones share the same 36 masonry finishes as Plinths and Spellstones. Stone Bricks use `vestige:standing_stone`; the other 35 use `<material>_standing_stone`.

**Presentation locked, October 6, 2026:** the owner approved all sixteen native models and their 36 masonry finishes after reviewing the in-game family. The [approved gallery and asset record](../art/standing-stone-native-v4/README.md) are the current visual baseline: shard signature selects shape and runes; the two matching body offerings select finish; same full signatures connect across finishes within one dimension. This approval concerns the model family and these established identity rules; ordinary XP payment is implemented, while alternate routes remain separately tracked below.

## Crafting and attunement

The accepted four-offering composition uses the October 8 ordered inner pattern **Attunement Shard → masonry → Ender Pearl → same masonry**, with an empty Spellstone reference surface and whole rotations equivalent. Outer nodes on a larger table are inactive. The two matching blocks select the result's cosmetic finish from the [shared 36-material palette](apparatus-materials.json). The original two-Chiseled-Stone-Bricks composition remains an alternative for the Stone Bricks finish. Mixing finishes, using slabs, or placing a double stack on only one surface does not supply the required two body offerings. No Paper is required for a device. The shard supplies network identity, the Pearl represents travel and the masonry supplies the body; these are native recipe roles, not inherited Iron properties.

The owner accepted **only the material in the Plinth carrying the Pearl** as the payment selector. Retain that payment route separately in the result. Other components do not require payment imbuement or stack payment discounts. All five material-selected payment routes are implemented; ordinary XP retains the owner-approved distance fare. See [payment rules](standing-stone-payments.md).

The ritual consumes one of each offering at the existing atomic commit point and returns one Standing Stone. It copies the shard's complete verified key. Device ingredients, local sockets and the device ritual's geometry do not rehash it. The four ingredients occupy the ordered inner pattern; outer seats are inactive and untouched. Changing an active offering or socket before commitment cancels without partial consumption. Installed imbuements remain in their sockets.

Craft several shards from the same reproducible blueprint, then use one per stone. Matching abbreviated rune/color marks help recognize related stones, but network membership compares all 256 key bits. Different keys cannot link through a coincidentally matching four-rune mark. Unattuned creative stones cannot join a network.

Rename the current stone directly in its destination menu, or rename its bound item in an Anvil before placement, to give its destination a useful label. Mining retains its attunement and name; placing the item creates a new endpoint ID at its new address. The shared key survives moving it. Keys are reproducible identifiers, not private ownership credentials: anyone able to use a stone can enter that network. Communication devices remain deferred.

## Signature-controlled appearance

Three independent inputs have distinct jobs:

| Input | Determines | Must not determine |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two matching body offerings | One of the 36 stone/masonry finishes | Network membership or payment |
| Full copied Attunement Shard key | Repeatable monolith silhouette and rune mark | Material finish or travel advantage |
| Pearl's Plinth imbuement | Stored travel payment route | Network key or silhouette |

The owner selected **ancient upright monoliths**, then explicitly rejected rectangular shafts with small added crown pieces. The accepted correction is variation through the **whole polygonal body**: angled sides, lean, taper, asymmetric shoulders, sloping crowns and changing thickness. Use a small shared family of coherent faceted stone shapes across every finish. The same key must choose the same silhouette and actual `AttunementMark` glyph/color combination after moving, reloading, or crafting with a different masonry finish. Do not roll fresh randomness, use world coordinates as a seed, or select from only the abbreviated tooltip mark. Different full keys may share the same silhouette and rune mark; such visual collisions never link networks.

This separation allows a Tuff stone and a Sandstone stone crafted from matching shards to share their crown/proportions and runes while fitting different builds. It also keeps 36 finishes from requiring 36 independently designed shape families. All silhouettes are cosmetic and must not supply discounts or power tiers.

[Eight faceted monolith concepts](../art/standing-stone-concepts/README.md) explore whole-stone profiles based on the owner's four photographic references. They are review art, not shipped geometry, textures or Minecraft screenshots. The first six rectangular/crown-only studies are rejected historical evidence; their one-to-three-cuboid construction must not constrain the redesign. The owner requested **sixteen native forms**, retaining Blade, Shoulder and Leaning and adding thirteen whole-body silhouettes. Each is one coherent faceted body, authored as a shared mesh and baked by NeoForge's native OBJ loader. Every finish uses its real vanilla texture tile without smoothing or copied mod assets. Four upright glyphs use the actual shard's `AttunementMark` letters/colors, rendered at full brightness on the front face. This makes the ink legible in darkness; it does not emit block light or imply shader bloom.

The sixteen forms are **Blade, Shoulder, Leaning, Spire, Wedge, Crown, Fang, Cleft, Hunched, Taper, Slant, Tablet, Chisel, Saddle, Obelisk and Keel**. All sixteen support all 36 finishes, giving **576 cosmetic form/finish combinations**. Whole-body meshes have 17–25 baked faces, retaining the previous maximum of 25. No decorative edge bands, collars or stacked caps are added. Per-form rune anchors keep the four glyphs inside the receiving face on shorter and leaning bodies. Meshes, anchors, collision bands, inventory predicates and the enum share the geometry authoring ledger.

The owner liked these three forms and requested slightly simpler edges. The front inset and angled perimeter band are removed; each profile retains its original outer outline and front/back depths. The direct front-to-side construction reduces each body from 34 to 25 baked faces (20/14 lower/upper to 15/10), about 26% fewer. [Native before-and-after views](../art/standing-stone-native-v2/README.md) use matching cameras, lighting, keys and finishes.

The owner's next accepted presentation adds a soft, slowly pulsing halo to the emissive ink and small copies of the stone's own signature glyphs drifting out and upward before fading. Both use the exact `AttunementMark` colors and letters. The stone body remains naturally lit; the ink and motes use full-bright native text with ordinary depth testing. Glow is visible in vanilla darkness without terrain lighting or shader bloom.

Motes are bounded client rendering, with at most three active trajectories per nearby stone. They are derived from world time, with no world entities, saved particles or server packets. Removing/unloading the stone removes its effect immediately. They render within 24 blocks; Minecraft's All/Decreased/Minimal particle options allow three/one/zero trajectories respectively. Minimal retains the luminous inscription. The body meshes and network identity are independent of the effect.

The sixteen-form expansion deliberately replaces the first prototype’s three-way modulo policy. The low four bits of the full copied key’s last byte select one of sixteen **fixed named buckets**, independently of enum length or masonry. Each bucket has equal opportunity for a uniformly distributed key. The original three inspection keys retain their forms; other old prototype keys may select a different form when newly placed. Existing network keys and rune marks are never rewritten. No migration is introduced; development uses fresh worlds. Future additions require another explicit owner-approved selection policy.

Each stone reserves a two-block vertical column and refuses placement beneath an occupied upper block or at the build-height ceiling. Facing follows the placer. The lower half alone stores the endpoint; either half opens its menu. Destroying either half removes both halves and returns one bound, named material item. Creative upper removal suppresses that drop. Collision and selection use conservative quarter-model-unit bands through the authored outline: this is a stepped voxel envelope, not exact triangle collision. All physical bounds remain inside the two occupied blocks. Inventory geometry uses the same two meshes joined at their original height.

## Selection and travel

**Selected layout, October 6: Flat List with native Minecraft buttons.** The centered menu is at most 210 GUI units wide. Destination buttons are always 20 GUI pixels tall, separated by two pixels; each server page contains at most six peers so a compact GUI does not squeeze rows. The current stone's name is centered in a shallow recessed panel, with a small unboxed pencil beside it; the bottom four-rune attunement mark uses a matching centered recess. Clicking the pencil reveals its name field and **Save**. Long header and destination names end in an ellipsis, with the complete name available on hover and in narration. The destination name sits on the left of each button; a whole-point charge followed by an XP-orb symbol sits on the right. There is no network heading, count, Current stone label, Signature label, Travel confirmation button or separate travel-cost line. Each unaffordable destination is disabled, and an affordable destination click immediately requests travel. Page controls and the page count appear beneath the list only when there are more than six destinations. A shorter list shrinks the panel; a source-only network retains just the matching name and signature panels. The source itself is represented once in the header and is omitted from destination pages. All other same-key, same-dimension members remain reachable through bounded six-entry pages. Payload registration version 4 requires matching client/server capacity without changing saved endpoint data.

The owner accepted **12 + 12 × sqrt(distance / 1,024) XP points, rounded once to a whole point**, as the first payment rate. Distance is three-dimensional between the stones. Prices come from server-authored destination data; enabled states follow current synchronized level/progress, and the server recalculates the fare and current affordability before charging. The ordinary XP implementation does not select health, hunger, mana or material discounts yet. The [preceding three low-fidelity proposals](../art/standing-stone-menu-sketches/README.md) remain historical design evidence; the selected preview now uses button rows with right-aligned costs. Native verification of the implementation is tracked in development status.

The existing name editor's **Save** or Enter commits a nonblank name up to 64 characters. The server removes unsupported text controls and trims outer whitespace. Blank, oversized and unchanged labels do not alter the endpoint. Renaming requires the same nearby, unexpired source session as travel and cannot target another or replaced stone. It updates the persistent directory and block entity without changing the endpoint ID, key, shape, finish or rune mark. The menu refreshes from server data after saving. Preserve these rules when making editing conditional on the pencil icon. No chat/actionbar message or floating world-name toggle is added.

The bottom attunement mark uses the same four colored runes as the shard and physical stone. It is a visual abbreviation; membership still uses the complete key. The source name remains available on every page. Paging preserves an unsaved name draft while the pencil editor is open. The [current fixed-row and counted-icon inspection](../art/standing-stone-icons-corner/README.md) records the narrower layout, ellipses, short/source-only lists, editing, all four six-row pages and direct paid XP travel. XP/mana amounts precede their symbols. Health/food costs show the actual full/half icon count (two native points per full icon), with exact underlying points in narration/tooltips. The shared [violet mana rune and horizontal meter](mana-display.md) appear in menu previews and the bottom-right HUD. Health, mana and hunger travel screens remain appearance-only with illustrative prices; their rates and payment selection are still deferred. The [preceding number-first review](../art/standing-stone-resources/README.md) and [inset review](../art/standing-stone-insets/README.md) remain historical. The [preceding native list](../art/standing-stone-list/README.md) and [first button-list pass](../art/standing-stone-button-list/README.md) remain historical evidence.

### Optional compatibility, deferred

The owner wants future Antique Atlas and Waystones compatibility while keeping the base mod independent. A separate addon is an acceptable future delivery option. No Atlas, Surveyor, Connector, UnionLib, Moonlight or Waystones dependency is introduced for the list menu.

Potential directions remain proposals: expose Standing Stone positions as optional Atlas markers; provide an explored-terrain destination view through a compatible map provider; or deliberately bridge selected Waystones interactions. Map markers and network membership are separate responsibilities. A future bridge must define access, discovery, payment and dimensional rules before mixing destinations; installing another mod must not silently merge its endpoints into native full-key networks or bypass either mod's travel rules. [Pinned map research](../research/standing-stone-maps.md) preserves API and loader evidence for a later prototype. Waystones APIs and the exact compatibility behavior have not been investigated or implemented in this change.

**Five payment routes are implemented:** empty Pearl socket keeps ordinary XP, Lapis Block reduces XP while also charging mana, Amethyst Block uses mana, Moss Block uses food, and Soul Sand uses nonlethal health. The approved rates, exact rounding and transaction behavior are documented in [Standing Stone payment imbuements](standing-stone-imbuement-plan.md). The departure alone selects payment; the unchanged shard key still defines network identity.

The server validates an open, unexpired session and a player still within eight blocks of the same source stone. Clients send source/destination IDs, never arrival coordinates or attunement keys. The destination must belong to the exact source network. A completed trip consumes the session; reopen the stone for another trip.

## Persistence and arrival

A world-owned directory retains endpoint ID, key, dimension, position and name across chunk unloads and restarts. Opening a menu does not load every destination. Selecting one loads its chunk for live validation, without a permanent chunk ticket. A removed/replaced endpoint is rejected and pruned if encountered as stale saved data. Breaking a loaded stone immediately removes it from the directory.

Travel uses the shared `NearbyTeleport` helper to randomly search positions one or two horizontal blocks from the target at its level or one block above/below. Open candidates require supporting ground, player-sized clear space, valid border/build height, and no fluid or recognized harmful floor/feet block. Every candidate is checked before the deliberately occupied fallback at the destination’s height is used. Missing destinations or unavailable in-bounds positions reject; crowded sites still permit travel. No terrain is changed. Departure and arrival use native portal particles and teleport sound.
