# Magical restoration objects: folklore research

**Researched October 8, 2026.** Research for the owner's proposed expensive repair catalyst: a durable item contributes some of its remaining durability to a damaged magical item during a Spellstone ritual. Its volatility can destroy the repair target. The successful output must preserve the target's complete identity and stored contents, changing only damage. The item name, recipe, restoration rate, durability, volatility and possible stabilizing imbuement are not settled by this research. No gameplay or assets are implemented here.

## Best material precedent: Nüwa's coloured stones

In the *Huainanzi*, chapter 6, Nüwa refines stones of five colours to repair the sky after a catastrophic breakdown of the world's order. The relevant clause is **女媧煉五色石以補蒼天**. This is a direct mythic precedent for specially prepared stone functioning as restoration material. It is not an account of a reusable handheld stone repairing tools. Source: [*Huainanzi*, “Lanming xun,” paragraph beginning 往古之時](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E6%B7%AE%E5%8D%97%E5%AD%90/%E8%A6%BD%E5%86%A5%E8%A8%93).

The *Liezi*, “Questions of Tang,” contains a related account. Lionel Giles's historical English translation describes coloured stone fashioned to correct defects in heaven and earth. His rendering uses “Kua” and masculine pronouns; it should be treated as a particular older translation, not silently harmonized with every later retelling. Source: [Giles, *Taoist Teachings from the Book of Lieh Tzŭ*, Book V](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Taoist_teachings_from_the_book_of_Lieh_Tz%C5%AD/Book_5).

**Vestige adaptation:** a costly fused mineral that restores the continuity of a damaged object. A multi-material crafting process has a stronger connection to this story than an ordinary rock that happens to heal equipment. Repeated uses, durability transfer, stabilization and explosions would be independently authored mechanics. The cited *Huainanzi* passage does not identify the individual five colours or prescribe a five-ingredient item recipe.

## Best rejoining precedent: Cormac's Cup

In *Cormac's Adventure in the Land of Promise*, Manannán demonstrates a decorated golden cup that breaks into three when three falsehoods are uttered over it, then becomes whole when truth is declared. The object is later called Cormac's Cup and is used to distinguish truth from falsehood. Source: [Whitley Stokes's 1891 translation, pp. 215–216, hosted by UCC CELT](https://celt.ucc.ie/published/T302000.html).

**Limit:** the cup restores itself; it does not restore another item or expend its durability. Its breaking is conditional on falsehood, not a random failure chance. The useful motif is a precious crafted object whose wholeness responds to the conditions of a ritual. CELT identifies the translation and manuscript witnesses; this does not establish the exact story as a documented ancient druid practice.

**Vestige adaptation:** restoration as reuniting the same object, rather than producing a newly standardized replacement. A name invoking concord or wholeness could fit that interpretation, but would be new game vocabulary.

## Useful bounded-restoration scene: Peredur's sword

In *Peredur the Son of Evrawc*, Peredur strikes an iron staple with a sword and breaks both. The fragments reunite after the first two blows. After the third, neither sword nor staple will reunite. His uncle explains the test in terms of Peredur having reached two-thirds of his strength. Source: [Lady Charlotte Guest's translation of *The Mabinogion*, “Peredur the Son of Evrawc,” the iron-staple episode](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/5160/5160-h/5160-h.htm).

**Limit:** this is a test of the hero, not an explicit two-charge repair item. It supports imagery of fragments becoming the original whole and of restoration having limits. A consumable repair budget would be a Vestige adaptation, not the stated explanation in the tale.

## Alchemical alternative: the philosophers' stone

Getty's collections include historical manuscripts depicting the preparation of the philosophers' stone, including Claudio de' Domenico Celentano di Valle Nove's *Book of Alchemical Formulas* (1606). Getty's curator also stresses the variety of historical understandings of the stone and discusses its association with the bonds of physical matter. Sources: [Getty, *Art of Alchemy*, with the manuscript identification](https://www.getty.edu/projects/art-alchemy/), [Getty curator Q&A on alchemy](https://www.getty.edu/news/your-questions-about-alchemy-from-its-origins-to-the-philosophers-stone-answered/).

**Limit:** these sources do not establish a standard philosophers' stone that repairs worn tools. This is broader material-transformation imagery, less direct than Nüwa's repair stones. It suits an expensive manufactured catalyst; it does not supply Vestige's exact use, price, depletion or volatility rules.

## Naming proposals and recommendation

These are **original Vestige proposals**, not recovered mythological artifact names:

**Later owner naming direction:** the item name must make its chaotic nature apparent. **Chaotic Restoration Stone** is the revised recommendation; the earlier names below preserve the initial research alternatives. The exact name remains unselected.

| Proposal | Reason to consider it | Tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Restoration Stone** | Clearest function; keeps the owner's stone direction and avoids the vanilla enchantment name. | Broad rather than distinctive. |
| **Rejoining Stone** | Emphasizes restoring the existing object and preserving its identity. | May suggest repairing shattered pieces rather than ordinary wear. |
| **Fivefold Stone** | A specific allusion to Nüwa's five-coloured restoration material. | Commits the identity to fivefold imagery; the exact name is invented. |
| **Concord Stone** | Suggests restoring agreement among the object's parts and offers vocabulary for stabilization. | Its function needs to be learned through use. |

**Research recommendation:** use Nüwa's coloured stones as the main material inspiration, with the same-object restoration motif supplied by Cormac's Cup. The initial **Restoration Stone** recommendation is now superseded by **Chaotic Restoration Stone**, which states both the restoration function and its chaotic risk. **Fivefold Stone** remains a historical thematic alternative rather than the current recommendation. The sources give no basis for claiming that a rechargeable, explosive repair stone with durability transfer is an authentic named artifact from folklore.

## Source limits

The *Huainanzi* Chinese text, the *Liezi* English translation, Stokes's Cormac translation and Guest's Peredur translation were inspected directly. On the linked Chinese Wikisource page, the heading is **卷六　覽冥訓** and the restoration paragraph begins **往古之時**; it contains **於是女媧煉五色石以補蒼天**. The retrieved page identifies revision **3933631**. The web tool's text search returned no match for Chinese terms despite that paragraph being visible when the page was opened directly; this does not indicate an absent passage. The Chinese Text Project copy was found but returned HTTP 403 when opened, so the readable Wikisource text is cited instead. Getty is the custodian and interpreter of its cited collection material; its contextual discussion is not treated as an original myth text. No particular in-game recipe, numerical balance or artwork follows automatically from these accounts.

**Naming selected October 8:** the owner selected **Fluxed Flint** for the original native volatile repair catalyst. Earlier candidate names above remain research/design history. Its implemented first-pass behavior and provisional balance are in [magical repair](../design/magical-repair.md).
