# Attuned device naming research

**Researched October 6, 2026; owner selections recorded later the same day.** The owner selected **Crane Bag**, **Whispering Shell** and **Homebound Eye**. Homebound Eye replaces the compass concept with handheld return teleportation; the owner also suggested a vanilla Spider Eye as a recipe ingredient candidate. See the [accepted device direction](../design/attuned-devices.md). The remaining names below are research alternatives, not accepted names; none of the three devices is implemented. Historical containers supply atmosphere and vocabulary, not evidence of the shared-inventory gameplay capability. **Runic Circle remains an internal/documentation term only and must not appear in player-facing UI.**

## Guidance vocabulary (superseded compass concept)

**Lodestar** is an attested English word for a guiding star, especially the Pole Star, and figuratively a guiding person or principle. Oxford records its Middle English origin as **lode**, in the obsolete sense of way or course, plus **star**. This is medieval English vocabulary, not a recovered ancient druid name for a compass. Source: [Oxford Learner's Dictionaries, “lodestar”](https://www.oxfordlearnersdictionaries.com/definition/english/lodestar).

Possible device names:

- **Lodestar**: reuse the historical word metaphorically for the instrument pointing toward its crafting site. The device meaning is a Vestige adaptation.
- **Lode Needle**: a newly coined compound using the attested older course/way sense of lode.
- **Hearth Needle**: a newly coined poetic name evoking a return home. It does not establish that the crafting location is a player's house.

The proposed compass points toward the actual place that crafted it. The reproducible attunement key alone cannot distinguish identical arrangements in different places; an eventual device would need a separate origin address. These names do not resolve whether moving or dismantling the original apparatus changes the destination.

**Whispering Shell** is the owner-selected name. It is newly authored; no cited source establishes that exact expression as a historical object name.

## Bag vocabulary

| Root or name | Attested meaning and context | Possible Vestige use | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Scrip** | An archaic small bag or satchel. Bosworth–Toller records Old English **scripp**, glossed as a bag, with a homiletic example; the American Heritage Dictionary records the present English noun as archaic. | **Attuned Scrip** is the strongest concise proposal: an old container word plus the mod's mechanism. **Kindred Scrip** is a more poetic proposal if the actual access rules justify kinship imagery. | These compounds are newly authored names. Neither the historical word nor its cited example implies magic or shared storage. |
| **Crane Bag** | An attested Irish legendary title: the library record for MacNeill's scholarly edition of *Duanaire Finn* lists “The Crane-Bag.” | **Crane Bag** supplies the clearest existing Irish legendary association. | It is specifically Irish Fenian literary material, not an established ancient druid implement. A modern game using the name would adapt the association. |
| **Corrbolg** | The Irish text of *Macgnimartha Find*, paragraph 16, contains **corrbolg na sed**; Meyer's parallel English translation calls the object a treasure-bag holding Cumall's treasures. | A source-language alternative, useful for lore if its spelling and pronunciation are deliberately adopted. | The accessed passage establishes a treasure container, not a shared inventory. **Crane Bag** is easier to understand in English UI. |
| **Pouch** | The Anglo-Norman Dictionary's **pouche** entry is dated approximately 1165 and includes bag/sack and purse/satchel senses, supported by medieval examples. | **Kindred Pouch** or **Attuned Pouch** gives the clearest everyday function with an older root. | The proposed compounds are inventions; the source is medieval Anglo-Norman, not Old English or ancient Celtic. |

Sources for **Scrip**: [Bosworth–Toller, “scripp”](https://bosworthtoller.com/27043), [American Heritage Dictionary, “scrip³”](https://www.ahdictionary.com/word/search.html?q=scrip+3). The latter identifies the word as archaic and supplies Middle English and Old French forms. The dictionary treatments should not be flattened into a claim that every modern form or sense was in continuous use from Old English.

Sources for **Crane Bag / Corrbolg**: [Irish Traditional Music Archive's catalogue record for *Duanaire Finn*, Part I](https://itmacatalogues.ie/Portal/Default/en-GB/RecordView/Index/80936), [Kuno Meyer's Irish text, paragraph 16, hosted by UCC CELT](https://celt.ucc.ie/document/G303023/), and [Meyer's English translation, paragraphs 16–17](https://celt.ucc.ie/document/T303023/). CELT identifies the text as medieval and its manuscript as Bodleian Laud 610; the Irish edition's introduction reports a 1453 copying date attributed to O'Donovan. This is a manuscript witness date, not proof of the story's original composition date. The [1908 MacNeill edition scan](https://archive.org/details/duanairefinnbook07macn/page/118/mode/2up) was located but could not be read through the available web access during this pass, so this note does not assert detailed contents, crane-skin construction, tide restrictions or exact composition dating from that poem.

Source for **Pouch**: [Anglo-Norman Dictionary, “pouche”](https://anglo-norman.net/entry/pouche), especially senses 1 and 2 and their dated quotations.

## Historical boundary and proposal

The examined evidence does not establish an ancient druid bag for exchanging possessions across distance. Medieval Irish legend, early English vocabulary and modern druid revival should remain separate provenance categories. The owner selected **Crane Bag**; **Attuned Scrip** and **Kindred Pouch** remain unused research alternatives.

For the ancient druid context, [Caesar's *Gallic War*, 6.14](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.02.0001%3Abook%3D6%3Achapter%3D14) describes the memorization of teachings and a restriction on writing them down. This is an ancient Roman account about druids, not a surviving druid-authored vocabulary. It supplies context for the limits of attributing modern fantasy item names to their practices.

**Earlier poetic shortlist (superseded):** **Lodestar**, **Whispering Shell**, and **Kindred Scrip**. Lodestar was a historical word proposed in a new compass sense; the other two were newly authored compounds. Subsequent owner decisions retained **Whispering Shell**, selected **Crane Bag**, and replaced the compass with **Homebound Eye**.
